The directing-point organizer — building the glide variations

Glide is the three-plane construction — picture, vanishing, directing — carried on the two frontal axes, sharing one projective depth law. The picture plane is fixed and the vanishing plane sits at the eye; what you organize is the directing point of each channel. Drag it out to infinity and that direction becomes central; slide it onto the vanishing plane and that direction goes parallel. That single move walks the whole family: pushbroom (one directing point on the vanishing plane), vanishing-axis glide (one out at infinity, one finite), one-point perspective (both at infinity). The right panel is the resulting projection on the tube; the yellow mark is where the depth-lines vanish — a point, an axis, or a window.

Every projection here is one placement of the directing points. The depth law is the same throughout — the reciprocal that the three planes encode; only the directing points move. Send both to infinity and the vanishing points of all depth-lines collapse to the single centre C: one-point perspective. Hold one at infinity and bring the other in, and they spread to a line: the vanishing axis. Slide one onto the vanishing plane and that whole direction stops converging: pushbroom. Pushbroom and one-point are corners of this space, not what glide reduces to — glide is the interior, and the directing points are how you steer it.
