The channel rates — how the two equations work

One reciprocal foreshortening runs the depth: w(z) = Zp/(Zp−z), worth 1 at the picture and falling to 0 at infinity — the shared station, the n→1/n law. Each channel then takes a fraction of it. The horizontal rate rᵤ sets the width scale sᵤ = (1−rᵤ) + rᵤ·w; the vertical rate rᵥ sets the height scale sᵥ = (1−rᵥ) + rᵥ·w. Each is a blend of staying true-size (weight 1−r) and foreshortening (weight r) — so on the graph each channel is a straight line, and the rate is how far it slides from true-size down toward perspective.

The two rates are the two coordinates of the atlas. rᵤ = 0 keeps width true-size (parallel, no convergence); rᵤ = 1 gives width the full foreshortening (perspective, converging to a point); between is the glide, where width crowds toward a residual scale of 1−rᵤ and stops. The vertical rate does the same for height, independently — and because the far scales are exactly (1−rᵤ, 1−rᵥ), choosing the depth frame’s width and height ratios is setting the two rates.
