The congruence map — where the ladder is a seam

The unit square is the space of double-slit congruences, coordinate (rᵤ, rᵥ). On the diagonal and the two banished edges the two readings fuse — the glide (edges) and the cross-slit camera (rays) draw the same lines — and that fused locus is exactly the top of the Erlangen ladder. Everywhere else the congruence is genuinely two-slit, the camera image leaves the homography group, and the readings split. The seam is the top of the ladder made visible: the diagonal rᵤ = rᵥ is the pinhole — a homography, the projective rung; the origin is orthographic, an affinity, the affine rung; the two banished edges are the pushbroom, one foot on each. Off the seam the congruence is genuinely two-slit: its camera image is no longer a plane self-map, and that is precisely the ground the Erlangen classification cannot see — which is exactly where glide and cross-slit split. The metric and Euclidean rungs do not appear on this chart; they are the calibrated overlay, a further choice of the circular points and of scale, layered on top of this purely projective picture.

Every point is classified with the shipped img₁ / img₂ / degen / ctype (DZ = 200, ε = 0.02); the split measure is splitDevMesh taken over the sample ray. So the chart agrees with the inversion viewer point for point — 0 on the seam, positive in the field.
