The midplane

A square face images square at two depths. Slide the picture plane toward the midpoint of the slits and watch the second one leave: it runs off to infinity, arrives, and comes back from the other side. At the midpoint exactly it is nowhere finite — and the shape returns to square at infinity in both directions instead. One slit now sits behind the picture plane, so no ray can reach the image having passed both. This is drawn, not photographed.

FACE SHAPE AGAINST DEPTH

House rule: no claim without a passing check. check_midplane.js holds the ledger (10/10): A(∞) = |z₂/z₁| always, so the shape is square at infinity exactly when the picture plane is the arithmetic midpoint of the slits; the finite square depth Zh is their harmonic mean and diverges as that midpoint is approached; and travelling from the scene the ray meets one slit, then the picture plane, then the other — it must pass the image before it has met both, so no exposure can realise this. Two means of the same pair: the harmonic one names a depth, the arithmetic one names a picture plane. check_invariance.js (8/8) settles which quantity is which: A(∞) = |z₂/z₁| is affine — it is anchored to the ideal point and moves when the picture plane moves. The invariant is (S₁, S₂ ; square, picture plane) = −1, preserved under any projectivity of the depth line. The map from picture plane to square depth is the involution with the two slits as its fixed points, exchanging their midpoint with the point at infinity — which is the midplane configuration, stated without coordinates.
