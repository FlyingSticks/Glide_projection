The directing-point organizer — construction, depth measure, projection

Glide is the three-plane construction — picture, vanishing, directing — carried on the two frontal axes and sharing one projective depth law. The picture plane is true-size (a square is a square); the vanishing plane sits at the eye; you organize the directing point of each channel. The left column is the construction over its depth measure — the reciprocal transfer that builds the depth ruler from the true-size metric ruler (depth = 1/n at perspective, uniform at affine, crowding toward the residual for glide). The right column is the resulting projection on the tube. Drag the directing points; the depth measure and the projection follow together.

The loop is closed: the directing points set the construction, the construction sets the depth measure, the depth measure is exactly the law the projection runs. Equal steps in depth image at reciprocal steps on the true-size picture plane — depth = 1/n — and moving a directing point reshapes that ruler: out to infinity gives the reciprocal (perspective), onto the vanishing plane gives a uniform ruler (affine, true size carried into depth), in between crowds the planes toward the residual 1−r. Pushbroom and one-point are corners of this space; glide is the interior, steered by the directing points.
