TETRA ENGINE · DRAFTING BOARD · GLIDE PROJECTION

One congruence, two readings — rays versus edges

Mats — the shortest honest path through this. You can build a perspective: rays through a station point, images where the rays cut the picture plane. Now keep the picture plane and put the station point away. What remains is a two-parameter family of sightlines — a congruence — and it can be read two ways. Read it as rays and you get a camera: the two-slit, every sightline threading a pair of axes at different depths. Read it as edges and you get a drawing: each line of the picture carries a graduation, and depth is a mark sliding along it. Same lines, two readings. They agree exactly on a seam — and the seam turns out to be the entire classical inventory: orthographic, pushbroom, pinhole, the affine and projective rungs of Klein's ladder. Step off the seam and the readings split. You are then holding a projection that draws in straight, graduated edges but is not a camera and does not live on the ladder. That object is the subject of this board. Nothing here is asserted on trust: every page re-runs its own machine checks as you drag.

The spine below is about an hour. Everything else is there when you want it.

CONTEXT I — THE AMBIENT LADDER

Klein classifies geometries by what their transformations preserve: Euclidean inside metric inside affine inside projective, three–four–six–eight degrees of freedom, cross-ratio as the crown invariant. The glide/cross-slit field is not a fifth ring. It overlaps the tower, and the overlap is exactly the seam — the classical world is the measure-zero locus where the two readings happen to agree.

CONTEXT II — RAYS VERSUS EDGES

One congruence over the ambient space, two readings of it. The ray reading gathers: sightlines converge through the slits to an image point. The edge reading graduates: the image rides a straight drawn line at a counted fraction of depth. Fused on the seam, split in the field — and the split is a drawable, measurable gap.

THE HARMONIC UNDERCARRIAGE

Why "graduated edge" is exact. The graduation is a cross-ratio, λ = ad/bc; its two self-dual values ±1 are the board's two balances — coincident reachable inside the frame, harmonic only by stepping outside. And the two readings themselves are harmonic conjugates of each other through the footprint pair, at every depth — which is why a straightedge converts one into the other.
