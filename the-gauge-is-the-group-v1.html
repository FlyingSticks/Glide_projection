The board doesn't just resemble the anharmonic interface — it runs the same group. Drag λ and watch its whole orbit move. The two “harmonic” identities you asked about — λ·(1/λ)=1 and 1/λ+1/σ=1 — are two different partners of the one λ; they meet only where the discriminant goes complex.

Why this is the real bridge, not the first one

My first guess put the two identities on one pair and forced λ = e±iπ/3. Wrong: the board keeps the partners apart, so that never happens on a real depth. The truer statement is stronger. The six maps {λ, 1/λ, 1−λ, 1/(1−λ), λ/(λ−1), (λ−1)/λ} are the anharmonic group S₃ acting on a cross-ratio — the exact object the Anharmonic Interface is built on. Its three involutions are the three the board constructs, and they compose as S₃: their fixed points are the six special values {−1, 0, ½, 1, 2, ∞}, and the order-3 products (the 3-cycles) fix the equianharmonic pair e±iπ/3. So the two projects aren’t analogous — they are the same S₃, once wearing depth-coordinates (the gauge), once wearing the cross-ratio (the interface).

Read it on the line above. At λ = 2 the coupling image lands on its own still point and the orbit collapses to the harmonic triple {−1, ½, 2}; same at λ = −1 (on the reciprocal’s still point) and λ = ½ (on the complement’s). Those three collapses are the harmonic case — one involution catching its fixed point. The equianharmonic is the deeper collapse, orbit of two, but it lives at e±iπ/3 and so never shows on the real line: the inset is the only place you can see where the reciprocal and the third involution finally agree. The colors are deliberate — the anharmonic involutions are drawn in the board’s own role-palette, because that identity is the whole point.
