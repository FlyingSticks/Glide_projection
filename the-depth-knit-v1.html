The depth-plane knit — scattered line elements, one depth

Four glide line elements, each with its picture, vanishing and directing point collinear at a fixed 3:1 ratio. Stretch, translate and orient them freely — the black picture→vanishing segment of each divides the same way (½, ⅔, ¾), the marks crowding toward its vanishing point. Because the same proportion is the same true depth on every line, connecting same-index marks knits a depth plane. Arrange them in plan (left) and the 3D view (right) builds the armature: the depth planes recede as frontal planes and converge on the vanishing plane, and the glide lines converge to the vanishing points and continue to the directing points. Try the vanishing-axis glide and one-point buttons, or drag the points to make your own.

Left = plan (drag the red/purple points to arrange) · Right = 3D view (drag to orbit) · the depth planes converge on the vanishing plane

This is the depth measure read the other way. Instead of setting a directing point and deriving the ruler, you place the line elements and let the shared proportional division do the work: dividing each picture→vanishing segment the same way secretly divides it by equal depth, so same-index marks land on a common frontal plane no matter how the lines are scattered. The plane is a quadrilateral in front, edge-on and parallel to the picture in side view, edge-on but differently shaped in plan — one depth plane, three appearances. But one ratio for every line means one isotropic glide: one-point is the corner where the directing point falls onto the vanishing point, while a vanishing-axis glide is anisotropic — its two channels direct at different depths, so it needs a split element (a directing point per channel), which is the two-channel organizer, not the single line element.
