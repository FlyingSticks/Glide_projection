The depth-plane knit — scattered line elements, one depth

Four glide line elements, each with its picture, vanishing and directing point collinear at a fixed 3:1 ratio. Stretch, translate and orient them freely — the black picture→vanishing segment of each divides the same way (½, ⅔, ¾), the marks crowding toward its vanishing point. Because the same proportion is the same true depth on every line, connecting same-index marks knits a depth plane. In the front view those connections are quadrilaterals; lifted into space they are frontal planes, parallel to the picture plane — orbit the right panel to see the lines diverge while the planes stay frontal. Share the directing point and the depth planes become affine copies of the picture points.

Left = front view (drag the red/purple points) · Right = the 3D lift (drag to orbit) · the coloured planes are equal-depth

This is the depth measure read the other way. Instead of setting a directing point and deriving the ruler, you place the line elements and let the shared proportional division do the work: dividing each picture→vanishing segment the same way secretly divides it by equal depth, so same-index marks land on a common frontal plane no matter how the lines are scattered. The plane is a quadrilateral in front, edge-on and parallel to the picture in side view, edge-on but differently shaped in plan — one depth plane, three appearances. Fix the 3:1 ratio and the whole family stays one consistent glide.
