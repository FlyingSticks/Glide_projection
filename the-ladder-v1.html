The ladder — projective, affine, vector, metric: structure as what a scramble cannot destroy

One scene, four kinds of scramble. The cast: four collinear points with their cross-ratio; a displacement arrow piercing a stack of leaves (gold rings count the crossings); the arrow's midpoint; the origin with two probe vectors and the unit circle. Each button shuffles the whole scene at once by a random map from one rung's repertoire — and the table on the right, recomputed honestly from the shuffled geometry, shows in green what that rung's maps can never destroy and in red what they just destroyed. Press rotate: everything holds. Press linear: length and angle die; the origin, midpoint, stack, count, cross-ratio hold. Press affine: the origin dies too. Press projective: the stack ceases to be a stack — leaves no longer parallel or evenly spaced, the midpoint slides — yet the pierce count and the cross-ratio hold even here, because they are pure incidence. The rungs going down are the board's Euler rings walked live.

Structure is whatever must be installed before a question has an answer; an invariant is a quantity no allowed shuffle can change. Each rung names an agreement about which shuffles are allowed: the projective rung allows any invertible map — only incidence, cross-ratio, and crossing counts survive, and this is the rung the drafting board lives on. The affine rung reserves a line at infinity: parallelism, evenly spaced stacks — the covector as a type of object — and midpoints become meaningful. The vector rung nails one point as origin: now displacements can be added and scaled against it. The metric rung installs the unit circle — a symmetric matrix g — and only then do length and angle exist, with rotations as the shuffles that respect them. Going down the ladder adds structure and shrinks the allowed shuffles; every older invariant survives on every lower rung. The pairing ⟨ray, stack⟩ = λ needs only the count — which is why the graduation works on a board with no scale bar, and why the vector/one-form reading of the two sides sits at the pre-metric layer of differential geometry: vectors and covectors exist and contract before any metric is chosen. The four rungs are the nested rings of the Euler plate; the scrambles are their groups, felt by hand.
