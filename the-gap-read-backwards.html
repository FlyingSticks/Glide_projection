The Gap, Read Backwards

The gap is the unit cell of a covector — the slab a reading of 1 spans. Described from the arrow side, it is the whole population of vectors scoring exactly 1. Hand over that population alone, with no levels drawn, and the instrument can be rebuilt from it.

Drag the marked head to choose a different member — the whole family follows, and so does the instrument it rebuilds. Spread tilts the direction along which the members differ from one another.

What the four steps show

1 · Population. Every arrow drawn shares a tail and scores exactly 1. Long and oblique, short and steep — the covector cannot tell them apart. No levels are on the page yet; this is all the evidence there is.

2 · Differences. Take any two members and subtract: the difference scores 1 − 1 = 0. Every such difference lies in the kernel, so the heads are collinear and the line they sweep gives the direction the levels run. The family's own internal spread names it.

3 · Spacing. Now one member is enough. Its tail sits on a level; its head lands on the next. Level 0 through the common tail, level 1 through the heads — one specimen fixes the width of the slab.

4 · Rebuilt. Even spacing does the rest: repeat the translation in both directions, without end, and the full ruling appears. The ascent needs no separate evidence — the population scores +1, not −1, so tail-to-head is already uphill.

Nothing is left over. Direction, spacing, and sign are all recovered, which is why the gap is not a property the covector has — it is the covector, described entirely from the arrow side.

One closure worth noticing: that population, drawn in the space of vectors rather than in space, is itself a level — the covector's own level 1, seen in the mirror. The kernel is the level through the origin; the scale is nothing but the decision about which parallel translate of it gets called one.
