C and D — the two cuts, and the two events

A segment PQ is cut at two points. C is the moving mark — the depth reading, the one that slides. D is the directing point — fixed by the channel’s rate. C makes the two gaps a = PC and b = CQ; D makes c = PD and d = DQ. The size ratio λ = ad/bc mixes one gap from each cut. Two special things can happen: coincident, when C meets D (λ = +1, and a = c, b = d — the cut goes to the center); and harmonic, when C and D are conjugates (λ = −1 — one cut inside PQ, the other outside).

coincidentC = D: the mark lands on the directing point. Then a = c and b = d, λ = +1, and the cut sits at the center — four equal quadrants (a square stretched to a rectangle keeps them equal).
harmonicC and D are harmonic conjugates: (P,Q;C,D) = −1. One cut is inside PQ, the other outside it (D beyond Q or before P, a negative gap). |ad| = |bc| with opposite sign.

This is the ground floor of the whole board: two cuts on a line, four gaps, one ratio. C carries the depth (it is the mark on the lateral channel, the image of a point at depth z); D carries the rate (it is the directing point, the crossing at 1/r). Coincident is C meeting D; harmonic is C and D straddling the ends of the segment as conjugates. Everything else — the cross-ratio page, the area square, the three costumes, the lateral projection itself — is built on these two cuts.
