Q and S as reciprocal images

The square on the radius never moves. Rotate R about M and the rectangle SM · QM changes shape without ever changing area — because it stays equal to R².

Slide Q toward the centre and S flies off to infinity; slide Q out to an anchor and S comes in to meet it — A and C hold fixed.

Shown, not asserted: MQ · MS = R² at every angle. The right angle at T is free (tangent ⊥ radius); dropping the perpendicular to Q makes MT the leg of right triangle MTS, and the leg theorem hands you MT² = MQ·MS — that is, R² = MQ·MS. The lavender rectangle is that product; the lavender square is R². They never come apart.

Two routes to Q. Circle route — tangent from S, right angle at T (tangent ⊥ radius), perpendicular to −Q; the leg theorem gives R² = MQ·MS. Rectangle route — no circle: the enclosing box carries the R² square and the SM·|QM| rectangle as the two complements about the gray diagonal, so Euclid I.43 makes them equal the instant M lands on the diagonal — they light up together as you drag. The magnitude |QM| is then swung onto the axis by the quarter-turn (the violet circle of radius |QM| about M), dropping onto +Q and −Q at once — the two signs of one square, (+1)² = (−1)² = 1. Switch to a single route to watch either proof alone; drag T or +Q to drive both.
