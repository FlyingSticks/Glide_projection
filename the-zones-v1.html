THE TWO EXTREMES ARE ONE PICTURE

Small square inside large square, four trapezoids between — the same eight image points, to the last decimal. Only the depth reading differs: on the left the small square is the far wall of a room you are standing inside; on the right it is the near face of a solid cube outside you. Zone A of the cross-slit produces the right-hand picture with a camera.

House rule: no claim without a passing check. check_xslit_zones.js holds the ledger (9/9): the closed forms Mx = z₂/(z₂−Z) and My = −z₁/(Z−z₁) are verified against the raw two-slit construction to 10⁻¹⁶; each channel's asymptote is its own slit; the face counts are 5, 3, 1 in the three zones; and a straight edge images as a genuine curve, which is why the receding edges below are drawn bent. check_isotropic.js adds the violet marker (5/5): |Mx| = |My| at the harmonic mean of the slit depths, at that depth only, always between the slits, and (z₁, z₂ ; Zh, 0) = −1. A physically built cross-slit has both slits between scene and sensor, so real hardware works in zone C only — zones A and B are in the family's geometry, not in its operating range.
