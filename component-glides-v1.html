The book’s two rules of assembly (p. 93): armatures join with no gaps or overlaps, and the whole image shares one infinity plane. This sheet builds the two canonical assemblies. The double herringbone (p. 97) is a vanishing-point armature between two herringbones — the middle axis of an eggcrate. The eggcrate (pp. 94–95) is that row crossed with its column: one point at the crossing of two vanishing axes, four herringbones on the edges, four boxes in the corners. The joints are the cell boundaries; the seat is the center vanishing point, anywhere inside the center cell.

The deep result: the assembly has no free parameters beyond the seat and the joints. The shared-staging-orthogonal rule forces every flank herringbone’s shear to u₀ − xjoint and every corner box’s drift to (u₀ − x, v₀ − y) — continuity is derived, not tuned. The tiled image of infinity shows it: axis segments and four congruent corner rectangles all meeting exactly at the seat. The red diagonal probe demonstrates p. 96’s eccentric effect live — a nonaxial transverse line bends where it crosses an armature joint, by exactly the slope ratio (1−g).

Exact law per channel, one gauge g(Z) = 1 − p(Z)/p(Zg): inside the center cell w = ŝ + (ŵ − ŝ)·(1 − g); outside it w = ŵ + (ŝ − joint)·g — rate-1 recession toward the seat between the joints, rate-0 parallel recession with the forced shear beyond them. The double herringbone is this law on the width channel only; the eggcrate applies it to both. Every cell reduces bitwise to a Sheet 39 core armature — the center to the vanishing point, the edges to herringbones, the corners to boxes — and the eggcrate with its horizontal joints pushed past the staging edges is the double herringbone, to machine precision. …
