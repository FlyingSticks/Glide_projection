Why they are the same object

Cut PQ at C and D and write the four directed lengths a = PC, b = CQ, c = PD, d = DQ. Put the four points on a channel line from the picture point (fraction 0) to the vanishing point (fraction 1): the mark sits at the graduation λ(z) = k/(k+1) with count k = −z/Zp, and the directing point sits at the crossing 1/r. Then the page’s quotient ad/bc is the four-point cross-ratio (P,V;X,D) — the genuine projective invariant — while the page’s product ac/bd is only the affine shape ratio. That quotient/product split is the projective / affine two-level structure the inversion carries throughout: the cross-ratio survives every Möbius map, the shape ratio only the affine ones. One channel shows it on four points on a line; the rate square shows it on two channels at once.

So the resemblance you noticed between the two squares is not a coincidence of shape. The cross-ratio page is the one-channel restriction of the lateral projection — one axis, four points — and its harmonic event is the armature’s 1/k + 1/c = 1 exactly. The rate square has a second dimension because it carries a second such channel on the perpendicular axis, at a possibly different rate; that is why the square’s interior (two independent rates) cannot be read off as a product of channel-wise conditions the way its boundary and diagonal can. You had built the seed of the whole two-level structure into a one-dimensional cross-ratio before the projection work made it two-dimensional.
