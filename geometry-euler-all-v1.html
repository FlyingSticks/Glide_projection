The tower and the field — one Euler diagram

The four classical geometries nest as groups. The double-slit congruence chart is not a fifth ring: neither contains the other. They overlap — and the overlap is exactly the seam.

One idea underneath — now with the outward step

Split the two channel rates — release the requirement that the picture map be a collineation at all — and you step off the ladder into the congruence field: no group, but the congruence and its drawn armature remain, and each channel still carries its own cross-ratio. Fuse the rates back (rᵤ = rᵥ, or send one to 0) and the ladder reappears — that fusing locus is the seam.

From there, every inner level is the projective plane plus a distinguished figure held fixed:

Fix a line — the line at infinity ℓ∞ — and you get affine geometry; the cross-ratio with the point at infinity collapses to the simple ratio.

Fix two conjugate points on it — the circular points I, J = (1 : ±i : 0) — and you get metric geometry: the angle between lines of slopes m₁, m₂ is θ = (1/2i) · log cr(m₁, m₂; i, −i) (Laguerre), and a circle is a conic through I and J.

Fix the scale and you get Euclidean geometry.

So the whole picture is one structure with progressively more pinned down — and the cross-ratio, which survives even in the field (one per channel), is what everything inward is built from.
