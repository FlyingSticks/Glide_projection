The theorem, in one line

Under the gauge, a level line keeps its length and reads count zero; a depth line keeps its count and its shadow is pure gauge. One noun, one verb, never both (Exclusivity, Thm 3.1).

Three experiments

1 — Spin the glide. The blue field writhes, every shadow readout spins, and every honest number sits at drift ≈ 1e−15. The red line does not feel the glide at all: the shear coefficient vanishes on the picture plane.

2 — Send a line to the post. Corollary 3.2: every depth line is congruent to the vertical post. Select any wild blue line and send it; the gauge spends itself making that one line canonical, and the rest of the field stays wild — relative invariants survive (watch |T| not move). Then send a different one. Each in turn can be the canon; none of them is.

3 — Twist the patch. Drag one corner of the green patch. The level rulings change length — the vector verb varying along the covector graduation, linearly (Thm 6.2) — and the diagonal bows with sagitta exactly |T|/4, live-checked. Then gauge hard: |T| and every ruling length hold still. Twist is relative content the gauge cannot touch.
