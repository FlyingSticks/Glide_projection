Does inside require minus?

No. Minus is what inside becomes when its two sides are twins, or when you choose to measure it as a signed field. Left alone, inside runs on order and multiplication — and never reaches for a sign. Drag the points and watch the fork.

the dividing inside

A point splits a line — the two sides are twins

Reflect across M and the left ray lands exactly on the right — same length, opposite label. The sides are congruent; nothing intrinsic tells them apart. Only a chosen direction does, and that choice is the minus. Here "which side" and "plus or minus" are one fact.

Drag P. The mirror twin (hollow) tracks it. Watch the sign flip as P crosses M.

the enclosing inside

A circle — inside and outside are not twins

Bounded ≠ unbounded — the two sides differ on their own, and no reflection swaps them (nothing carries a finite region onto an infinite one). The inside↔outside map is d ↦ R²/d: every quantity positive, pure multiplication. No minus is ever reached for.

Drag P inside toward M and its image flees outward; drag it out and the image comes in. The product stays R².

where they marry — by choice

The neutral surface — inside made signed

Off, you have two regions and no minus — inside in its bare form. On, you choose to grade them as one scalar: the surface becomes zero, the regions become + and −, and crossing flips the sign. Inside didn't ask for the minus. You did.

Toggle the field on, then drag the probe across the surface.

So the dependency runs backwards from how it's usually asked: to have a minus you already need a distinguished zero and a chosen positive side — an oriented inside. Minus presupposes inside; bare inside presupposes only order. Left to itself — a bounded interior, betweenness, the reciprocal on the circle — inside gets by without a sign.
