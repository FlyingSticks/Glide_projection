How the instrument works

There is one shared configuration: four red near corners, four black far corners, and the dashed ⊙, all draggable in the panel at lower left. Every station on the arc reads this same state and recomputes its glyph and its measure on every drag. The map of the suite has become a synoptic gauge: one input, five simultaneous readings.

The wiring encodes the suite's argument. Stations v2, v3, and v4 read the black corners exactly as they stand — general position. Stations v1 and the dial are the pinhole readings: they ignore the black corners and instead snap the far face toward ⊙ at ratio 0.82, because that is what each of those pages assumes. So dragging a black corner moves three stations and leaves two untouched, while dragging ⊙ does the reverse — the split between assumption and freedom is drawn as circuitry, not stated as a caption.

The three measures

⊙ spread (v2) extends the four depth edges to lines and takes the maximum pairwise distance among their six meets — a metric quantity, in pixels. When the edges are parallel the meets vanish and the best it can say is ∥.

Δλ (v3) sections the four depth edges with the four face diagonals — both diagonals of the near face and both of the far — and takes the cross-ratio of each cut in the fixed order (TL, TR; BR, BL). A pencil has a single λ on every transversal (verified to 2×10⁻¹⁵), so Δλ, the maximum pairwise difference across the four cuts, measures how badly that invariant splinters. Note the order convention matters: relabeling the edges sends λ through its six-member anharmonic orbit, which is exactly where the dial picks it up.

bow (v4) treats each side panel as the bilinear cell Q(s,t) = A + (B−A)s + (D−A)t + T·st with twist T = A−B+C−D. The rulings (rings and depth lines) are straight; the on-surface diagonals Q(s,s) and Q(s,1−s) bow off their chords with sagitta exactly |T|/4 (verified to 3×10⁻¹⁴ over 2000 random cells). T = 0 precisely when the cell is a parallelogram, i.e. the panel is flat — the Type A / Type B line from the third sketch sheet. The glyph draws the two green surface diagonals of each of the four panels.

λ (the dial) is the cross-ratio of the pinhole pencil itself, computed at ⊙ from the direction vectors to the four near corners: λ = (d₀×d₂)(d₁×d₃) / (d₀×d₃)(d₁×d₂). Its six orbit members {λ, τλ, ρλ, σλ, ωλ, ω²λ} ride the mini-axis in the role colors; the verdict flips to HARMONIC when λ lands in {−1, 2, ½}.

The test signals

twist restores the default general position — far face deliberately knocked off the collector configuration, all three measures nonzero. pinhole snaps the black corners onto the ⊙ ray at ratio 0.82: spread collapses to 0, Δλ to 0, and the arc's readings all agree with the dial. prism translates the far face rigidly, making the four depth edges parallel: the metric check fails outright (∥, no meets) while Δλ still settles to 0 — verified to 10⁻¹⁶ — because parallels are a pencil through an ideal point; and the bow drops to exactly 0, since translated panels are parallelograms. This one preset is the whole argument for changing currency: the projective reading survives where the metric one dies, and the affine reading (bow) flags the flatness. harmonic searches the panel for a point of a Chasles conic and moves ⊙ onto it, snapping the far face along — the pencil's λ lands in the harmonic orbit and the dial says so.

Pricing

Spread is metric-grade and dies at infinity. Δλ is projective-grade and survives; its vanishing on four cuts is necessary for concurrency and, by pencil invariance, the honest sampled form of the full criterion. Bow is affine-grade — it measures the twist defect, not incidence. Chasles (fixed cross-ratio ⇒ ⊙ on a conic through the corners) is theorem-grade and is what the harmonic signal exploits. The arc of the suite is these three currencies in ascending order of what they survive, and the dial branch is what becomes measurable once the currency is cross-ratio.
