What this is

The Type A transfer sheet, made solid. A closed polygon in the picture plane (red, ratio 1) and a matched partner in the back plane (blue, ratio 0) span a compound of doubly-ruled panels — one hypar per edge, sewn along the vertex depth lines. Level slices interpolate vertexwise; the green web in the top view is the sheet's nested slice family, and the purple bows are each panel's curved diagonal.

Why "volume" is a licensed word

The apparatus has no depth metric, but every gauge element has determinant ±1, so the slab carries an honest volume form dA∧dh. Slice areas are the vector verb, the count is the covector verb, and volume is their pairing one dimension up: V = ∫A(h) dh. Because vertices move linearly in the count, A(t) is exactly quadratic, so the prismatoid formula V = (A₁ + 4A½ + A₀)/6 is an identity of the apparatus, not an approximation — live-checked in the ledger against a 64-panel integral (residual ≈ 1e−15).

Three experiments

1 — Spin the glide. The whole volume writhes and leans; V, all three areas, and the warp census hold at drift ≈ 1e−15. The lean readout spins: a single volume's lean through depth is pure gauge.

2 — Drag one red vertex. A₁ and V move; A₀ sits still. The two ends of the slab are independent vector data joined only by the covector transport. Then slide the ratio and watch the slice sweep between them along the parabola.

3 — Read the circuits. Around any closed compound the face twists telescope: ΣT⃗ = 0, the closed circuit of twists — warp circulates but cannot accumulate. Each face still keeps its own |T|/4 sagitta (the purple bows), and their census is honest.
