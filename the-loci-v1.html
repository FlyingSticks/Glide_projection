This closes the loop that began with two squares that seemed to rhyme. The cross-ratio Λ on your lateral channel has exactly two self-dual values, and they are the fixed points of the complement-swap Λ ↔ 1/Λ — the same involution whose factorization organizes the inversion (reading 1 ↔ reading 2). Now they have addresses in the plane: +1 is an interior line, −1 is an exterior hyperbola. That single geometric fact — one locus inside, one outside — is why, all the way back in the C/D sheet, coincident was reachable by a mark meeting a directing point while harmonic demanded stepping out of the segment. The awkwardness that broke every earlier drawing of the harmonic case was never a notation problem; it was this: the harmonic locus is a curve that cannot enter the rectangle. The free point, the signed cells, and the two loci make that visible at last.
