The two balances — the mark, the directing point, and the two planes

This is where the whole thread closes, tied back to the armature. The frame’s two opposite edges are the PICTURE plane (left & top, the P side) and the VANISHING plane (right & bottom, the Q side). The mark C is a vertical cut; the directing point D is a horizontal cut. Their four gaps are a = C→picture, b = C→vanishing, c = D→picture, d = D→vanishing, and λ = ad/bc. The two cut lines cross at a point; that crossing sits on the picture→vanishing diagonal when λ = +1 (coincident, inside the frame) and on a hyperbola outside the frame when λ = −1 (harmonic). This closes the loop that began with two squares that seemed to rhyme. The cross-ratio Λ on your lateral channel has exactly two self-dual values, and they are the fixed points of the complement-swap Λ ↔ 1/Λ — the same involution whose factorization organizes the inversion (reading 1 ↔ reading 2). Now they have addresses in the plane: +1 is an interior line, −1 is an exterior hyperbola. That single geometric fact — one locus inside, one outside — is why, all the way back in the C/D sheet, coincident was reachable by a mark meeting a directing point while harmonic demanded stepping out of the segment. The awkwardness that broke every earlier drawing of the harmonic case was never a notation problem; it was this: the harmonic locus is a curve that cannot enter the rectangle. The free point, the signed cells, and the two loci make that visible at last.
