The split element — the fully general glide

When both channels are finite and unequal, the directing structure splits: each glide line carries two directing points, one per channel — the u-directing point at λ = 1/ru and the v-directing point at λ = 1/rv along picture→vanishing. They land on two perpendicular axes, and the four vanishing points form a quad. Dial the two rates and watch the special cases fold back: equal rates collapse both directing axes to one centre (isotropic); a perspective channel merges its directing axis into the vanishing points (vanishing-axis); both perspective sends everything to the centre (one-point).

This completes the family. A single-ratio line element carries one directing point and reaches the isotropic glides and the ones with a perspective or affine channel. The general glide needs the split element: two directing points per line, on two perpendicular axes at two depths in the relief. The isotropic glide is where those two axes merge to a point; the vanishing-axis glide is where one of them merges into the vanishing points; one-point is where the vanishing points themselves collapse to the centre. One depth law throughout — only the directing structure changes, and the split element is its most general form.
