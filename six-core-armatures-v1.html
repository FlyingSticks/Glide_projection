The plate on p. 93 names six core lateral armatures — vanishing point, vanishing plane, vanishing axis, herringbone, keystone, box. This sheet runs all six through one law: each channel of the picture contracts toward its seat at its own rate, driven by a single depth gauge g(Z) = 1 − p(Z)/p(Zg) — the vanishing-diagonals device of pp. 77–78 in closed form. The rates tell the whole taxonomy: (1, 1) a point catches infinity; (λ, λ) infinity arrives early as an area; (1−k, 1) the vanishing axis with its directing axis, 1/k + 1/c = 1; (0, 1) the herringbone — vanishing fans one way, parallel fans the other; (0, λ) the keystone, a compressed herringbone whose would-be axis ghosts to a directing axis; (0, 0) the box, a compressed paraline.

Same scenes, same gauge square, same drop-an-.obj pipeline as Sheet 38. What to reach for: the herringbone for long, narrow, shallow fields — street facades and aerial corridors (p. 80); the vanishing axis for the most unified pictorial field (p. 88); keystone, box, and the two point forms mostly as parts awaiting the component glides of pp. 92–97. All six are shallow-space instruments — the deep corridor stays perspective’s.

Exact law per channel: w = ŝ + (ŵ − ŝ)·(1 − r·g) + σ̂·g, with staging ŵ the true image at Zg and one gauge g(Z) = 1 − p(Z)/p(Zg), p(Z) = −Zp/(Z−Zp). At r = 1 the seat is the image of ∞. At 0 < r < 1 infinity arrives at (1−r) scale and the seat goes dashed beyond — a directing seat at gauge 1/r. At r = 0 recession is parallel; only the staging shear σ acts, and it “does not affect the accuracy” (p. 89).
