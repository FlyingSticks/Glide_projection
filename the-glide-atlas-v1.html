The glide atlas — the whole family on one square

Every projection in the family is a point in the (ru, rv) square — the two channel rates. The diagonal is the isotropic glides, the r = 1 edges are the vanishing-axis glides, the r = 0 edges are pushbroom and affine, the corners are one-point / orthographic / pushbroom, and the whole interior is the general glide. Drag the marker: the armature on the right shows the directing structure, and the table below highlights where you are. Two things never change — the picture plane is true-size, and one reciprocal depth law runs through all of it. Only the directing structure moves.

Invariant everywhere. The picture plane is true-size — a square is a square, at every point of the square. And one projective depth law runs through the whole family: equal depth steps divide picture→vanishing at the same proportion λ = k/(k+1), so the cross-ratio is preserved — depth = 1/n at perspective, its reciprocal face elsewhere. Only the directing structure changes from point to point.

Read the atlas as one statement. The picture plane anchors the metric; the depth law fixes the recession; and where the directing points sit — collapsed to a centre, strung on one axis, or split across two — is the single steering wheel that turns perspective into orthographic, pushbroom, the vanishing-axis glide, and the fully general glide. The single line element covers the diagonal and the edges; the split element covers the interior. That is the entire family, corner to corner.
