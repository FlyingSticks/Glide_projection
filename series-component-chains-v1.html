Series configurations “are patterned into linear repeating or nonrepeating sequences of lateral glide armatures” and “often make use of diverging lateral armatures” (p. 92). This sheet is the general chain: five cells along one vanishing axis, each with its own rate — converge (1), keystone rate (0<r<1), parallel (0), or diverge (r<0), the fans opening toward a frontal focus. The rules of assembly (p. 93) hold by construction and by live enforcement: continuity leaves no gaps or overlaps at the joints, and the legal rates form the window 1/g(Zƒ) < r ≤ 1, so the width map stays monotone for every depicted depth, all the way through infinity.

The deep result again, now propagating: C⁰ continuity at each joint forces the next cell’s intercept — cᵢ₊₁ = cᵢ + (rᵢ₊₁ − rᵢ)·jᵢ — so one anchor plus the rates and the joints determines every seat and every shear in the chain: the readout lists the seats the construction forced, not seats you chose. A corollary the sliders will show you: a truly periodic repeating series must alternate converging with diverging cells — which is precisely why the book says series glides “often make use of diverging lateral armatures.” The freeform hybrids of p. 98 are held for a companion sheet.

Exact law: cell i carries drift Dᵢ(V) = cᵢ − rᵢ·V, and u = V·p(Zg) + Dᵢ(V)·(p(Zg) − p(Z)); heights recede at rate 1 toward the axis. Anchor: D(s) = σ. Every cell’s fans concur at its seat sᵢ = cᵢ/rᵢ at gauge 1/rᵢ — on the axis when r = 1, beyond the infinity plane when 0 < r < 1, and in front of the staging when r < 0 (the diverging focus). Equal-rate neighbours make their joint vanish identically, which is why the double-herringbone preset reduces to Sheet 40 to machine precision. Framing note: a chain’s reachable set is unbounded, so this sheet frames the current drawing exactly rather than the whole slider travel. …
