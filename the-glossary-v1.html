TETRA ENGINE · DRAFTING BOARD · GLOSSARY

The glossary — key terms of the board

The working vocabulary of the spine and every plate before the annex, in Kevin's own terms, with the page of Glide Drawings a term is drawn from where there is one.

Two words carry the rest. A projection is a line congruence given a role; the two roles are rays (which gather to an image) and edges (which graduate along a drawn line). Everything below hangs off that one distinction and the inversion that swaps it.

1 · The central move

The foundations shared by every sheet. Learn these six and the rest of the board reads itself.

Line congruence
A two-parameter family of lines filling space — the raw material a projection is built from. The catalog the board uses is small: the parallel bundle, the pencil through a point, and the two-axis (double-slit) family. the introduction · the congruence map
Projection — a role, not a formula
A congruence given a role. The same congruence read one way flattens the scene; read the other way it shapes a solid. Six classical constructions become one structure seen from different sides. the introduction
Rays
The role that gathers: sightlines converge — through slits or a centre — to an image point. The family that flattens. In the sheets this is reading 2, the cross-slit / camera side. context II · the inversion viewer
Edges
The role that graduates: the image rides a straight drawn line at a counted fraction of depth. The family that shapes the solid. This is reading 1, the glide side. context II · the inversion viewer
The inversion
The organizing move of the whole board: swap the role on one and the same congruence. Read as edges it is the glide; read as rays it is the cross-slit. One congruence, one drawn armature, two graduations. the introduction · the inversion viewer
Armature — the drawn armature
The lines you actually draw: picture points, vanishing points, directing points. It is the orthographic shadow of the congruence, and it is shared by both readings whichever way you read it. throughout
The seam — the fused locus
The measure-zero set of configurations where both readings draw the same lines. It is exactly the top of the classical (Erlangen) ladder — the small place where the two graduations coincide. the congruence map
The field — the split locus
Everywhere off the seam, where the two readings genuinely differ. The difference is a drawable, measurable perpendicular gap, not a rounding error — the field's charted height stays ≥ 0.49. the congruence map
Fixed points vs dual pairs
Two ways the classical projections fall out of the role-swap. Fixed points: both readings agree as full maps (a classical camera). Dual pairs: one drawn armature carries two different projections. the introduction · the inversion viewer

2 · The ambient ladder

Klein's tower of geometries, and where the board's field sits relative to it — beside it, not inside it.

Erlangen program — Klein's ladder
Classifying geometries by what their transformations preserve: Euclidean ⊂ metric (similarity) ⊂ affine ⊂ projective — that is, E(2) ⊂ Sim(2) ⊂ Aff(2) ⊂ PGL(3). the geometry tower · the tower and the field
The dof ladder 3·4·6·8
The degrees of freedom of those four groups, rung by rung — the machine-verified spine of the tower plate. the geometry tower
Cross-ratio
The crown invariant, preserved by projective maps and everything below them. On a graduated channel it shows up as λ = ad/bc, the board's depth law. the geometry tower · the bridge
Circular points / Laguerre
The two points at infinity fixed by every similarity; Laguerre's formula recovers an angle as a cross-ratio against them (stated exactly, verified to 10⁻¹⁶). the tower and the field
Not a fifth ring
The congruence field neither nests inside nor contains the classical tower. It overlaps it, and the overlap is exactly the seam — the classical world is the measure-zero locus where the two readings happen to agree. the tower and the field
The rate square — the congruence map
The unit square of double-slit congruences, coordinate (rᵤ, rᵥ) = the two channel rates. Diagonal: pinhole. The r = 1 edges: vanishing-axis glides. The r = 0 edges: pushbroom and affine. Corners: one-point / orthographic / pushbroom. Interior: the general glide reached by no camera. the congruence map · the glide atlas

3 · The projections

The six classical cameras that sit on the seam, and the constructed region that lies off it.

Orthographic
Parallel projection: the origin of the rate square. Reaches into the affine band of the tower, and is the source of true-size / true-length views. the congruence map · the assembly
Perspective — pinhole, one-point
The pencil through a single centre; the far corner of the square, sitting in the projective band. Deep scenes — corridors — still belong here. the congruence map · the tower and the field
Pushbroom
The unique flat lateral that still reads depth: its width channel is a one-form (a stack), its height channel a pencil. Straddles the affine/projective boundary; the r = 0 edges. the tower and the field · the assembly
Cross-slit
The ray reading of the two-axis congruence: two slits at two depths on one axis of sight, every sightline threading both. glide vs cross-slit · station point to vanishing axis
Glide / lateral projection
The edge reading of the same two-axis congruence — a convergent shape drawn under rectilinear projection. (The board renames "glide" to "lateral" after the lateral armature; both terms appear across the sheets.) glide vs cross-slit · the bridge
Vanishing-axis lateral
A station point pulled apart into two perpendicular armature axes — a vanishing axis that meters depth and a directing axis that assigns direction. station point to vanishing axis · Glide Drawings pp. 88–90
Real / ideal / constructed
Three grades of projection. Real: all-finite meeting sets (perspective, cross-slit). Ideal: exact limits (orthographic, pushbroom). Constructed: the generic lateral with bent rays — not a camera, and not a limit of cameras. the introduction
One-form — the depth stack
A channel read as evenly-spaced planes rather than lines through a point: equal λ steps are equal depth (ζ) steps. The width channel of the pushbroom and the lateral. the assembly
Pencil
A family of lines through one common point — perspective's raw material, and the height channel of the pushbroom. the assembly · the tower and the field
ζ — depth
Depth measured from the picture plane backward. The variable the channels graduate. the assembly · the inversion viewer
True size / true length / edge-on
The orthographic view relations: a frontal face reads in true size, a body diagonal in true length, side elevations go edge-on. the assembly

4 · The harmonic undercarriage

Why "graduated edge" is exact: it is a cross-ratio of four cuts, with two self-dual balances at λ = ±1.

C — the moving mark
The depth reading: the cut that slides as depth changes (C = X). It makes the gaps a = PC and b = CQ. C and D · the bridge
D — the directing point
The cut fixed by the channel's rate, assigning direction. It makes the gaps c = PD and d = DQ. C and D · the bridge
The four gaps
On the segment PQ: a = C→picture, b = C→vanishing, c = D→picture, d = D→vanishing. One from each cut on each side. C and D · the two loci
λ = ad/bc — the size ratio
The cross-ratio of the four cut-points, mixing one gap from each cut. This is the channel's depth graduation. C and D · the bridge
Picture plane / vanishing plane
The frame's opposite edges: the picture plane (left & top, the P side) and the vanishing plane (right & bottom, the Q side). the two loci
The four cut-points
One channel's whole armature: picture point P, the mark C = X, the directing point D, the vanishing point Q = V. The very same four points the cross-ratio cuts — which is why the cross-ratio page is the lateral projection, one axis at a time. the bridge
Coincident — λ = +1
The balance where C meets D. Reachable inside the frame; the +1 locus is a line through the frame's interior. C and D · the two loci
Harmonic — λ = −1
The balance where C and D are conjugates, one inside and one out. Reachable only by stepping outside the frame; the −1 locus is a hyperbola lying wholly outside it. Equivalent to |ad| = |bc|. the hinge · the two loci
The two loci
The drawn homes of the two balances: +1 a line through the interior, −1 a hyperbola outside — the geometric reason harmonic can only be reached by stepping out. the two loci
The three costumes
One equation |ad| = |bc| in three guises: matched size (equal complement-rectangle areas — Euclid I.43), similar shape (equal diagonal-rectangle aspect ratio), and straight corner-diagonals (the two corner-diagonals through the inner point line up). The sign of λ tells the two balances apart. the hinge
Harmonic conjugates — of the two readings
The two readings are harmonic conjugates of each other through the footprint pair, at every depth — which is why a straightedge alone converts one into the other. the harmonic undercarriage · the conjugate construction
The conjugate construction
Recovering reading 2 from reading 1 with a straightedge only: fix the base pair {+X footprint, −X mirror}, put the mark in, read the camera image out through a quadrangle ("cat's-cradle") — no measurement, no division. It is an involution: swing the scaffold and the answer doesn't move. Formally, cr(img₁, img₂; +X, −X) = −1. the conjugate construction · the assembly

5 · The engine room

The one law the plates run on, the six armatures it drives, and the component assemblies built on them.

Channel law — s(r, z)
The engine every sheet runs on: each channel of the picture contracts toward its seat at its own rate r as depth z grows. the six core armatures
Depth gauge — g(Z)
The single meter behind the channel law: g(Z) = 1 − p(Z)/p(Zg), the vanishing-diagonals device of pp. 77–78 written in closed form. the six core armatures · Glide Drawings pp. 77–78
Seat / rate / shear — σ
Seat: the point a channel contracts toward. Rate r: how fast it contracts. Shear σ: the herringbone tilt — σ = 0 is the flat (pushbroom) chart. the six core armatures · component glides
The six core armatures
Six patterns of recession run by the one gauge: vanishing point, vanishing plane, vanishing axis, herringbone, keystone, box. The channel rates give the whole taxonomy. the six core armatures · Glide Drawings p. 93
Rate taxonomy
What a rate means per channel: converge (r = 1), keystone rate (0 < r < 1), parallel (r = 0), diverge (r < 0, the fans opening toward a frontal focus). series component chains · the glide atlas
Rules of assembly
Two: armatures join with no gaps or overlaps, and the whole image shares one infinity plane. component glides · Glide Drawings p. 93
Double herringbone
A vanishing-point armature set between two herringbones — the middle axis of an eggcrate. component glides · Glide Drawings p. 97
Eggcrate
A herringbone row crossed with its column: a single point at the crossing of two vanishing axes. component glides · Glide Drawings pp. 94–95
Forced-shear continuity
The joining constraint that makes shear match across a component seam, so herringbone and eggcrate cells assemble without a break. component glides (Sheet 40)
Series component chains
Linear repeating or nonrepeating sequences of lateral armatures, often diverging: for example five cells along one vanishing axis, each carrying its own rate. series component chains (Sheet 41) · Glide Drawings p. 92
Dynamic closure floor
The lower bound r > 1/g(Zf) that keeps the width map well-behaved for the current scene and station — the admissible band g(Zf) < r ≤ 1. series component chains (Sheet 41)
Infinity plane
The single plane at infinity shared by an entire assembly — one image, one infinity plane. component glides · series component chains
Frontal focus
The point diverging fans open toward, at negative gauge (r < 0). series component chains
The glide atlas
The whole family laid on one (rᵤ, rᵥ) square: the diagonal is the isotropic glides, the r = 1 edges the vanishing-axis glides, the r = 0 edges pushbroom & affine, the corners one-point / orthographic / pushbroom, and the interior the general glide. the glide atlas

6 · Reading the ledger

How the board separates what is proven from what is argued — the discipline the introduction keeps.

The assembly
One scene point S carrying every structure at once, in 3D: picture plane, depth stack, both slits, the camera ray threading them, and the graduated edge with its mark. The place to orbit and watch fused become split. the assembly
Machine-verified vs synthesis
The introduction's two grades of claim: results proven in a check script, versus framing and synthesis that is "true as far as the checks reach, revisable in its framing." The two carry different weight on purpose. the introduction
Check scripts
The numbered, dependency-free scripts that guard each claim's arithmetic and internal consistency (check_crossratio 6/6, check_klein 5/5, and the rest). They guard arithmetic, not aim — they cannot catch proving the wrong thing, and that loop is human-cranked. throughout · the introduction
Seam gap / split measure
The measured perpendicular distance between the two readings off the seam — the field's charted "height." Seam gap ~10⁻¹⁴; field ≥ 0.49 where charted. the congruence map
Definitions are compiled from the pages themselves and keyed to Glide Drawings where a construction has a canonical page. Where a term names a synthesis rather than a checked result, it inherits the introduction's caveat — accurate as far as the sheets reach, and revisable in its framing.
